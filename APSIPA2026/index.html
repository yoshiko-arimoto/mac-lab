Overview

This page introduces some example of recorded laughter samples and those synthesized laughter generated by the model introduced in our research paper.

Abstract: This study proposes a speaker-specific corpus of spontaneous Japanese conversation for laughter synthesis. A major issue in laughter synthesis is the limited number of laughter resources, which interferes with high-quality synthesis while preserving speakers’ individuality. To address this issue, daily conversations among five specific speakers were recorded over a four-month period. To collect laughter expressed in natural interactions while maintaining high recording quality, two recording setups were employed. Field recordings were conducted in the speakers’ everyday environments to reduce the unnaturalness and psychological bias associated with laboratory settings, whereas laboratory recordings were conducted in a soundproof room to collect high-quality laughter recordings while suppressing noise such as crosstalk and reverberation. By including these two recording setups, a corpus consisted of an average of 20.4 hours of conversational speech per speaker, providing both natural interactions and high-quality audio recordings. A preliminary synthetic evaluation with laughter in a field-recorded subset for two speakers revealed the highest naturalness score for the male speaker among the compared corpora, whereas the result did not generalize to the selected female speaker. These findings suggest that the corpus is a promising resource for speakerspecific laughter synthesis and that recording quality and speakerdependent laughter characteristics are important factors in future corpus design and evaluation.
